# T Level Legal Services: Syllabus Map (Crime, Criminal Justice and Social Welfare)

Course context: Level 3, 2 years, full time, 16-18. Delivered by Wigan & Leigh College (Parsons Walk Campus), where it is listed as "Law & Criminal Justice T Level". No learner personal details are stored in this repo.

## 1. Qualification structure

- Awarding organisation: Pearson, with CILEX as sector partner.
- Technical Qualification (TQ) has two mandatory components:
  1. Core component: an externally set exam on core knowledge, plus an employer-set project on core skills.
  2. Occupational specialism: Legal Services Assistant, Crime, Criminal Justice and Social Welfare.
- Industry placement: about 45 days (315 hours), separate from the TQ. Must be relevant to legal services.
- English and maths are built in, with a requirement to work towards grade 4 if not already held.
- Overall grades: Pass, Merit, Distinction, Distinction*. Distinction* needs an A* in the core and a Distinction in the specialism.
- UCAS points: D* 168, D 144, M 120, P (core C or above) 96, P (core D or E) 72.

## 2. Core knowledge (exam)

### 2.1 Route core (Legal, Finance and Accounting)

- C01 Business environment: role of business in society; public, private and voluntary organisations; business models and structures; stakeholders; confidentiality; public sector structure and funding; decision-making and ethics; drivers of change (PESTLE).
- C02 Company law basics: Companies Acts, articles of association, directors' duties, shareholder rights, penalties for non-compliance.
- C03 Tax basics: direct and indirect tax, key UK taxes, how tax law is made.
- C04 Careers and routes in the professions: solicitor, barrister, chartered legal executive, licensed conveyancer, probate practitioner, paralegal and technicians; private practice vs in-house vs public sector (CPS, councils, NHS, Government Legal Service); qualification routes (degree, apprenticeship, professional qualifications).
- C05 Regulation: SRA, BSB, CILEX, CLC, FCA, PRA, FRC, TPR; information vs advice; regulated vs unregulated products; anti-money laundering and financial crime; case studies (confidentiality, data protection, money laundering, PPI).
- C06 Professionalism and ethics: professional conduct; confidentiality vs disclosure; abuse of position; codes of conduct; ethical dilemmas; sanctions (warning, fine, suspension, disbarment); governance; professional scepticism; maintaining competence.
- C07 Security and risk: privacy and confidentiality; cyber security (encryption, phishing, insecure connections, cloud); data protection; conduct risk; fraud prevention.
- C08 Equality, diversity and inclusion: Equality Act 2010, Human Rights Act 1998, protected characteristics, vulnerable clients, unconscious bias.
- C09 Professional services: third parties (insurers, lenders, experts), professional indemnity, service standards and SLAs.
- C10 Fundamentals of law (route level): legislation and common law, devolved legal systems, role of courts, tribunals and Parliament, consumer protection (Consumer Rights Act 2015), international influences.
- C11 Fundamentals of financial accounting: types of financial data, elements of accounts, profit and loss, cash flow, solvency, sources of finance, basic bookkeeping and business maths.
- C12 Technology: digital transformation, automation, case management systems, e-filing (Companies House), fintech, AI, online dispute resolution, changing roles.
- C13 Data and design thinking: data architecture, data governance, big data, analytics and visualisation.
- C14 Research skills: source reliability, fact vs opinion vs bias, primary and secondary research, plagiarism, paraphrasing, referencing.
- C15 Project and change management: project structure and reporting (for example Gantt charts), roles, planning and control, quality/cost/time, records, evaluation.

### 2.2 Legal Services pathway core

- L01 Professional privilege: the concept of legal professional privilege.
- L02 Legal research: sources of law, legal reasoning, using online tools consistently with duties to the client.
- L03 Online and virtual courts (civil and criminal), from start to finish of a matter.
- L04 Case management systems: client confidentiality, conflicts, client money, data protection.
- L05 English legal system: court hierarchy for England and Wales, common law, binding precedent, civil vs criminal, sources of law.
- L06 Judicial review basics: scope, limits, nature of the test, process, remedies.
- L07 Criminal law basics: criminal court hierarchy, life of a case from arrest to appeal, trial process, sentencing range, corporate criminal liability, tax evasion, Criminal Finances Act.
- L08 Contract law basics: formation, terms, defective contracts, illegal/void/voidable, ending contracts, breach, limitation periods, remedies (goods and services, employment, business relationships, residential property).
- L09 Tort basics: duty of care, breach, causation, vicarious liability, remedies, Civil Procedure Rules pre-action protocols, ADR (mediation, arbitration), defences, legal costs.
- L10 Legal services sector: market overview, where law is practised, contentious vs non-contentious work, third-party professionals.
- L11 Money handling: client account rules, client vs office account, billing, billable vs non-billable hours, fixed fees, legal aid.

## 3. Employer-set project (core skills)

Assessed as a project against a brief set by an employer. Assessment objectives: plan the approach, apply core knowledge and skills, select techniques and resources, use maths/English/digital skills, deliver an outcome and review it against the brief.

Core skills demonstrated:
- P1 Research and analyse an area of law and apply it to a situation (for example a briefing document for a supervisor).
- P2 Communicate clearly to lay and professional audiences, orally or in writing.
- P3 Work collaboratively (for example draft a timeline of client information and missing documents).
- P4 Apply an ethical approach to client needs (for example discuss ethical issues in a set of instructions).
- P5 Show compliance with professional regulation (for example a memo on where client money should be held and why).

## 4. Occupational specialism: Crime, Criminal Justice and Social Welfare

Organised as three performance outcomes (POs). Each has knowledge topics and practical skills.

### PO1 Assist with the inception, progression and completion of legal services

Criminal knowledge:
- S1.01 Police procedure and interviews: PACE 1984, no-comment interviews, identification procedures.
- S1.02 Crown Prosecution Service: charging standards, Code for Crown Prosecutors.
- S1.03 Private prosecutions (HSE, local authorities, insurers, Environment Agency, RSPCA, fire authorities, commercial bodies, individuals).
- S1.04 Criminal Procedure Rules (basic); key legislation such as the Proceeds of Crime Act.
- S1.05 Practitioner texts: Archbold, Blackstone's Criminal Practice, Stone's Justices' Manual, Wilkinson's Road Traffic Offences.
- S1.06 Court requirements (postal requisitions, production orders); general defences.
- S1.07 Parole Board: constitution, jurisdiction, procedure, powers; Parole Board Rules 2016; recall (fixed-term and standard).
- S1.08 Custody time limits; probation service and pre-sentence reports.
- S1.09 Witness summons; special measures (automatic and on application); retrials; defendant appeals from the Crown Court (sentence and conviction); Attorney General's references.
- S1.10 Sentencing options: custodial, community, financial, discharges, ancillary orders (for example sexual harm prevention orders); victim's code and witness charter.
- S1.11 Legal aid, access to justice, eligibility.

Social welfare knowledge:
- S1.12 Housing law: Housing Acts 1985, 1988 and 1996, Equality Act 2010; homelessness (eligibility, priority need); anti-social behaviour legislation (2014 Act); third parties (housing associations, Housing Ombudsman, social services, landlords); organisations (National Landlords Association, Equality Advisory Support Service, Shelter).
- S1.13 Welfare benefits: Universal Credit, housing benefit, jobseeker's allowance, overpayments.
- S1.14 Debt law: Consumer Rights Act 2015, Insolvency Act 1986; Insolvency Service; insolvency practitioners; bankruptcy, Debt Relief Orders, IVAs; secured creditors and charging orders; mortgage possession; Money Advice Service; Financial Services Register and FCA; Citizens Advice.

Skills:
- Explain to a client what happens from arrest, detention and interview to charge and first appearance.
- Take outline instructions and refer to the right adviser.
- Review and summarise evidence, offences and possible defences for a supervisor.
- Explain identification procedures, and first appearance, trial and sentencing milestones.
- Outline strengths and weaknesses of the prosecution case before a plea hearing.
- Take accurate attendance notes; use legal texts for case management; prepare jury bundles.
- Apply the disclosure test to unused material; apply the Parole Board test for release or move to open conditions.
- Take instructions from pre-sentence reports for mitigation; outline grounds and time limits for appeal.
- Assess legal aid and benefit eligibility.
- Housing: take preliminary instructions (possession claim, landlord dispute, housing discrimination); advise on court procedure and homelessness decisions, review and appeal.
- Debt: take preliminary instructions; check the Financial Services Register; give first-step advice (bailiffs, debt recovery, court action); assess benefit eligibility; explain court processes (fine, CCJ, repossession order); explain priority debts, secured vs unsecured; explain alternatives to bankruptcy (DRO, IVA, consolidation, DMP).

### PO2 Carry out legal and factual research and present findings orally and in writing

Knowledge:
- S2.01 Navigating criminal practitioner texts in detail, plus sentencing resources (Banks on Sentence, Current Sentencing Practice).
- S2.02 Online resources: CrimeLine, LexisNexis, Westlaw, BAILII; online data sources.
- S2.03 Sentencing Guidelines Council and guidelines; ancillary orders and case law (Criminal Behaviour Orders, Sexual Harm Prevention Orders, Restraining Orders).
- S2.04 Prison Service Instructions; prison adjudications (internal and external).
- S2.05 Housing: Housing Acts, Landlord and Tenant Act 1985. Debt: Limitation Act 1980, Insolvency Act 1986.

Skills:
- Identify whether a charge is summary, either way or indictable, and the impact on venue, sentencing and costs.
- Find and cite current sentencing guidelines, key sentencing case law and ancillary orders.
- Identify statutory defences for a criminal and a regulatory offence.
- Locate and apply Prison Service Instructions/Orders.
- Use online data to support a client's account or defence statement.
- Identify the responsible person or company in regulatory crime (fire safety, health and safety, environmental).
- Housing: mandatory and discretionary possession grounds; defences to possession; disrepair claims and counterclaims.
- Debt: outline all debt solutions (repayment and insolvency) with written advice on benefits and costs; identify limitation periods.

### PO3 Draft legal documents and standard communications for review by a supervisor

Knowledge:
- S3.01 Criminal Procedure Rules, Legal Aid Rules, Parole Board Rules; Osborn, Booth and Reilly (2013) and other Parole Board case law.
- S3.02 Disclosure: Criminal Procedure and Investigations Act 1996. Witness statements: s9 Criminal Justice Act 1967. Special measures.
- S3.03 Client care letters (terms of engagement) and file notes; funding sources (client, public).
- S3.04 Housing: Housing Acts 1985 and 1988, local authority tenancy powers (introductory and secure), judicial review in housing.
- S3.05 Debt: Insolvency Act 1986, IVAs (criteria and impact).

Skills:
- Draft client care and file storage letters; complete legal aid forms; draft client care letter for a privately paying client.
- Draft a proposed indictment or information from templates.
- Answer the section 18 Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 questions and draft the response.
- Draft orders from templates (Sexual Harm Prevention Order, Criminal Behaviour Order, Restraining Order).
- Draft applications from templates (Parole Board oral hearing, special measures).
- Draft disclosure request letters; letters requesting legal aid transfer; straightforward witness statements.
- Draft a public-release summary of a Parole Board decision.
- Housing and debt: client care and advice letters; chronologies for court; witness statements; standard court forms (small claim for debt or rent arrears); notices (end of tenancy, seeking possession); judicial review pre-action letter; debt action plan; schedule of incomings, outgoings, assets and debts.
